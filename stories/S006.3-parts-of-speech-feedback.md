# User Voice

As a child, I want to see which words I got wrong after my second go, and the right answer after my last go, so that I can fix my mistakes myself and learn from the ones I couldn't fix.

# Use Cases

## UC1 - Red marks after a wrong second try

- After a wrong second try, the server tells the page which words are wrong and which are missed, as well as the counts S006.2 already gives.
- Every wrong word turns red (`#dc2626`) where he can pick it up from. For a word in the wrong row, that is its place in the table. For a missed word, it is the word in the sentence.
- Words he sorted correctly stay black, and he can still move them.
- A red word stays red while it is dragged. When he drops it somewhere other than where it was on the second try, it turns royal blue (`#4169E1`), as a changed box does in skip counting. "Somewhere" is a row of the table, or back in the sentence when the drop is outside the table. If he puts it back where it was on the second try, it is red again.
- Only the red words change colour. A black word stays black wherever he moves it.
- After a reload before the deadline, the red words are shown red again where they were on the second try.

## UC2 - The answer after a wrong third try or a timeout

- When a sentence finishes wrong, the server tells the page the answer: the part each word belongs in, or none. It also says, for each listed word, whether he had it in the right row, had it in another row, or did not sort it, and which words he sorted that belong in no row.
- A wrong third try shows "Not quite. Here is the answer." in place of S006.2's "Not quite." A timeout shows "Time ran out. Here is the answer."
- The table now shows the correct answer. Each row lists the words that belong in it, in sentence order, all in black text, with these backgrounds:
  - pale green (`#dcfce7`) for a word he had in this row
  - pale red (`#fee2e2`) for a word he had in a different row
  - pale orange (`#ffedd5`) for a word he did not sort
- A word he had sorted that belongs in no row, such as `the` under `noun`, is shown struck through on pale red in the row he put it in, after that row's correct words.
- The sentence goes back to plain black text with nothing red.
- The answer stays on screen while "Next Question" is showing, as S006.2 describes: greyed out for 3 seconds, then the button or Enter moves on, or the next sentence appears on its own after 15 seconds.

# Acceptance Criteria

The examples use the fox sentence from S006.1, with nouns `fox` and `dog`, verb `jumped`, and adjectives `quick`, `brown`, and `lazy`.

- [ ] Two wrong tries with `fox` in `verb` and `dog` left out turn `fox` red in the `verb` row and `dog` red in the sentence. The correctly sorted words stay black and can still be moved.
- [ ] A red word stays red while dragged and turns royal blue when dropped somewhere else, including back in the sentence. Dropped back where it was on the second try, it is red again. A black word stays black wherever it is moved.
- [ ] Reloading after a wrong second try and pressing Start shows the red words again in the same places.
- [ ] After a wrong third try with `fox` in `verb`, `dog` left out, and `the` in `noun`:
  - the `noun` row shows `fox` on pale red, `dog` on pale orange, and a struck-through `the` on pale red
  - the `verb` row shows `jumped` on pale green
  - the `adjective` row shows `quick`, `brown`, and `lazy` on pale green
  - all of it is in black text, the message says "Not quite. Here is the answer.", and the sentence is plain black
- [ ] A timeout shows the same kind of answer with "Time ran out. Here is the answer."
- [ ] The answer stays on screen until "Next Question", Enter after the first 3 seconds, or the 15-second wait moves on.
- [ ] In the browser's developer tools, which words are wrong and missed appears only in the response to a wrong second try, and the answer appears only once the sentence is finished.
- [ ] The red and blue colours and the answer display are checked in a real browser with real mouse drags.
