# User Voice

As a parent, I want to write capitalisation sentences into named pools that tasks draw from, so that one pool can feed
several tasks with different timeouts and pass marks.

As a child, I want to click the words in a lowercase sentence that need capitals, and see which ones I got wrong at
the end, so that I learn where capitals go.

# Behaviour

## Capitalisation pools

Pools are YAML files in `capitalisation_pools/` beside the root policy file. They are stored, named, loaded, and
identified as parts of speech pools are in S031.1. A pool has `name`, `level_of_difficulty`, and `sentences`. Each
sentence is either a string, or a mapping with `sentence` and `level_of_difficulty`.

```yaml
name: Names and places
level_of_difficulty: 1
sentences:
  - On Tuesday, Mia and I flew to Sydney.
  - sentence: Dr Patel works at NASA in the United States.
    level_of_difficulty: 1.5
```

A **word** is a run of characters between spaces. Punctuation stays part of its word, so `Sydney.` and `Mia's` are
each one word. A word **has a capital** when any of its letters is upper case.

- [ ] `dw check` accepts the pool above when a `capitalisation` task names it, and loads only the pools that at least
      one `capitalisation` task names, as in S031.1.
- [ ] Each of these fails the load and names the pool, and the sentence where there is one: two pools with the same
      name, a pool with no sentences, an empty sentence, a sentence with no capital, the same sentence twice in one
      pool, or a `level_of_difficulty` outside 0.5 to 10.

## Task body

A `capitalisation` task's `body` has `pools`, `timeout`, `correct_answers_required`, `minimum_questions`, and
`maximum_questions`.

- [ ] The fields, their defaults, the runnable size, and the load errors for bad values are as in S031.1's task body,
      except that `timeout` defaults to `60s` and there is no `parts_of_speech` filter.
- [ ] A `capitalisation` task is offered, chosen, given up, and counted toward today's revision like other tasks, and
      a passed or failed attempt starts its cooldown as in S006.

## The question

- [ ] Each question is drawn, timed by its level of difficulty, and counted toward pass and fail as in S031.1's
      Selection and timeouts and Pass and fail. With the default `60s` timeout, the `level_of_difficulty: 1.5`
      sentence above gets 90 seconds.
- [ ] The sentence is shown in large text, all in lower case, with the instruction "Click the words that need a capital
      letter". The first word and `I` are lower case too.
- [ ] Moving the mouse over a word that has a letter shades it light grey. A word with no letter, such as `—` or `3`,
      is not shaded and cannot be clicked.
- [ ] Clicking a word shows it as the pool spells it, in blue. Clicking it again puts it back in lower case and black.
      `nasa` becomes `NASA`, `sydney.` becomes `Sydney.`, and `mcdonald's` becomes `McDonald's`.
- [ ] Clicking a word that has no capital in the pool, such as `flew`, shows it with only its first letter capitalised,
      `Flew`, in blue.
- [ ] The timer bar, sounds, points shown, and "Try a different task" work as in S006.2.

## Tries and the hint

- [ ] He submits with a large Submit button or Enter. Submit and Enter do nothing, and use no try, when no word is
      clicked or nothing has changed since his last wrong try.
- [ ] A sentence is right when the words he clicked are exactly the words with a capital in the pool. He has three
      tries, worth 1, 0.5, and 0.25 points.
- [ ] After a wrong first or second try, the page says how many clicked words have no capital in the pool and how many
      words with a capital he has not clicked, such as "2 words shouldn't have capitals. You missed 1 capital. Try
      again." A count of zero leaves its sentence out, and a count of one says "1 word shouldn't have a capital". His
      clicked words stay as they were, and nothing is marked red.

## The answer

- [ ] After a wrong third try, or when the timer bar empties, the page shows the answer. Every word is shown as the
      pool spells it. A word with a capital he clicked is blue. A word he clicked that has no capital, and a word with
      a capital he did not click, are red. Every other word is black. A timeout scores no points, and the answer marks
      his clicks as they stood when the bar emptied.
- [ ] A right answer shows the sentence as the pool spells it, with all its clicked words blue, and moves on as in
      S006.2.
- [ ] "Next Question" and Enter are ignored for the first 3 seconds of the answer.
- [ ] A reload before the deadline brings back the same sentence with the tries he has used, his last submitted clicks,
      and their hint, as in S006.2.

# Notes

Depends on S031.1 for pools, difficulty timeouts, selection, and the end conditions, and on S006.2 and S006.3 for the
shell, tries, sounds, and answer display. Reuse the pool loader and the parts of speech run code rather than copying
them, so that the two task types keep the same rules.

Clicking toggles between lower case and the pool's spelling, so a word such as `iPhone` or `NASA` is right only in the
pool's form. A word clicked by mistake still has to look like a capital to him, which is why a word with no capital in
the pool shows only its first letter capitalised.

Comparing words by position avoids any trouble with a word that appears twice, such as `the` in "The cat saw the dog".

The child's page needs a mouse, as the parts of speech page does. There is no keyboard way to click a word.

Out of scope: the portal pages, the attempt page, and the MCP tools, which are S040.2. Until S040.2 is done, the parent
reviews an attempt through `/attempts` and the parent attempt list.
