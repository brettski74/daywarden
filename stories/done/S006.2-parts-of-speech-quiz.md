# User Voice

As a child, I want to pick a word out of the sentence and drop it into the row for the part of speech I think it is, and move it again or put it back if I change my mind, so that sorting the words feels like a game rather than a form.

As a parent, I want each sentence to be worth less on every try, I want the server rather than the browser to decide whether he sorted it right and in time, and I want a quiz that ends without enough points to count as a failure, so that a passed quiz stays away for weeks and a failed one comes back soon.

# Use Cases

## UC1 - Runs and sentence order

- A run asks every sentence in the task once. At the start of each run the sentences are shuffled into a new random order.
- His points are the sum of the points for the sentences finished in the current run. The sentence number is the number of sentences finished plus one.
- The one-hour expiry from S004.1 applies. Coming back more than an hour after his last activity starts a new run on the same attempt, with zero points, a new order, and the task settings in force then. A sentence that was open when the run expired is closed as a timeout first.
- An attempt uses the task settings stored on it when its run started, as in S004.1.

## UC2 - Start screen and layout

- The start screen works as in S004.1 and S011. It says "Parts of speech" and shows his points out of the number of sentences, the pass mark, and the sentence number, such as "0 / 10 points", "7 points to pass", and "Sentence 1 of 10". The Start button is focused, and Enter or a click starts. No timer runs on the start screen. It is there so that his first click unlocks the browser's audio.
- The top of the question screen shows his points out of the number of sentences, the pass mark, and the sentence number on the left, such as "2.5 / 10 points · 7 to pass · Sentence 4 of 10". The small "Try a different task" button is on the right. Points show quarter points as `.25` and `.75`, and whole points with no decimals, as in S011.
- Points are always shown out of the number of sentences, because each sentence is worth up to 1 point. The pass mark is shown beside them, never as the number they are out of.
- A heading says "Sort the words into the table".
- The sentence is shown centred in large text, about 2.5 times the page's normal font size. A long sentence wraps onto more lines. It never needs sideways scrolling. Punctuation is shown exactly as written.
- Under the sentence is a table with one row for each part of speech in the sentence's `parts`, in the written order.
- The left column is narrow, just wide enough for the longest part name. The right column takes the rest of the width. Each right-hand cell starts empty, and is at least as tall as one line of the sentence text, so an empty row is easy to drop into.
- Under the table is a line for feedback messages. Under that is a large "Submit" button.
- A timer bar along the bottom shrinks from full to empty over the time left on the sentence.

## UC3 - Picking up a word

- A word that he has not sorted gets a light grey background (`#e5e7eb`) while the mouse is over it, so he can see both that it can be picked up and which word a click will pick up. The pointer shows that it can be grabbed. The shading goes when the mouse moves away. Punctuation and spaces never highlight.
- Otherwise the words in the sentence look the same whether they are sorted or not: plain text with no background.
- A word that is already sorted does not highlight and cannot be picked up from the sentence. Its copy in the table is the only one that can be moved.
- Pressing the mouse button on a word and moving the mouse starts a drag. A press and release without moving does nothing.
- When a drag starts, the light grey shading stays on the word in the sentence for the whole drag, so he can see which word he picked up. A copy of the word appears exactly over the word and moves with the mouse, keeping the same offset from the pointer. The copy has a transparent background, so it never hides the shading of the row it passes over. The word in the sentence stays where it is, and the sentence never changes shape.
- The browser's own text selection and drag image do not appear while dragging.
- The two `dry` words in "Dry the dishes with a dry towel." are separate words. Each can be sorted on its own, and each has its own place in the sentence.

## UC4 - Dropping a word

- While he drags over a row of the table, that whole row turns light blue (`#dbeafe`), so he can see where the word will go if he lets go now. Only one row is highlighted at a time. The row returns to normal when the pointer leaves it.
- Releasing the mouse over a row, including over its label, sorts the word into that part. The row's background returns to normal. The word is added to the end of that row's right-hand cell, which lists its words in the order they were dropped, separated by commas. The words wrap within the cell. The word is shown as it is written in the sentence, with its capital letter if it has one. The shading on the word in the sentence goes.
- Releasing the mouse anywhere outside the table, or outside the window, makes the copy fly back to its word in the sentence. The flight takes about 1 second and eases into place. When it lands, the copy disappears into the sentence word, the shading on the sentence word goes, and the word is not sorted.

## UC5 - Moving a sorted word

- While the mouse is over a word in a right-hand cell, that word and its twin in the sentence both get the light grey background. He can see which word he would pick up, and which word in the sentence it came from. Both lose the shading when the mouse moves away.
- Pressing the mouse button on it and moving the mouse starts a drag, as from the sentence. A copy with a transparent background moves with the mouse. The word stays where it is in its cell, and both it and its twin in the sentence keep their shading for the whole drag.
- The word does not leave its cell until the mouse button is released somewhere else. Releasing it drops both shadings straight away:
  - On a different row, the word leaves its first cell, and the commas there close up. It is added to the end of the new row.
  - On its own row, nothing moves. The copy flies back to the word's place in the cell, taking about 1 second as the flight to the sentence does, and disappears into it.
  - Outside the table, or outside the window, the word leaves its cell and the copy flies back to its twin in the sentence, as in UC4. It is then not sorted, and can be picked up from the sentence again.

## UC6 - Submitting

- Clicking Submit or pressing Enter sends the table to the server as a try. Enter does this wherever the focus is, and is the same as clicking Submit.
- Submit is greyed out and does nothing, and Enter does nothing, when:
  - the table is empty
  - the table holds exactly the answer from his last wrong try on this sentence
  - a word is being dragged or is flying back
  - a try is waiting for the server's response
  - a "Correct!" message or "Next Question" is showing
  - the "Try a different task" confirmation is open
- As soon as he changes the table after a wrong try, Submit works again.
- The server refuses the same submissions without using a try. It also refuses a fourth try, a word that is not in the sentence, a part that is not in its table, a word in two parts, and a try for a sentence that is not open. Neither of the first two refusals applies to a submission sent because the timer ran out.

## UC7 - Judging a try and the feedback

- A wrong word is a word he sorted into a different part from the one the task lists it under. That includes a word the task lists under no part, such as `the` sorted into `noun`.
- A missed word is a word the task lists under a part that he has not sorted.
- The try is correct when it has no wrong words and no missed words.
- A correct try scores 1 point on the first try, 0.5 on the second, and 0.25 on the third. It shows "Correct!" under the table and plays a sound from `correct/`. After about 1 second the next sentence appears on its own, or the end screen (UC10) after the last sentence. Nothing can be dragged during that second.
- A wrong first or second try plays a sound from `incorrect/` and shows a message with the two counts, such as "2 words are in the wrong row. You missed 1 word. Try again." A count of zero is left out: "You missed 2 words. Try again." or "1 word is in the wrong row. Try again." The message uses "word" or "words" to match the count. Nothing on the page shows which words are wrong. Every word stays where he put it, and he can move any of them.
- A wrong third try scores 0, plays a sound from `incorrect/`, and shows "Not quite." It finishes the sentence (UC9).
- S006.3 adds red marks after a wrong second try and the answer after a finished wrong sentence.

## UC8 - Timer

- A sentence's deadline is the moment the server issued it, plus its `timeout` from S006.1, plus the 0.5-second network allowance from S004.1. The deadline does not move for tries, messages, or a reload. The timer bar shows the time left to that deadline.
- When the timer bar reaches zero, the page sends whatever is in the table, marked as from the timer running out, even when the table is empty or unchanged. A word being dragged at that moment is dropped back where he picked it up from, without the flight. If a try is waiting for its response, the timer's submission is sent as soon as that response arrives, as in S011.
- A submission from the timer running out that is correct scores the points for the try he was on: 1, 0.5, or 0.25.
- Anything else the timer running out sends is a timeout. It scores 0 points, plays a sound from `timeout/` and never one from `incorrect/`, shows "Time ran out.", and finishes the sentence (UC9).
- A try the server receives after the deadline is a timeout whatever it holds. Its placements are still recorded.

## UC9 - Moving on after a wrong sentence

- After a wrong third try or a timeout, nothing can be dragged and the timer bar is hidden. The table stays as he left it until S006.3 shows the answer there.
- A "Next Question" button replaces Submit. For the first 3 seconds it is greyed out, and the button and Enter do nothing, so a burst of Enter presses does not skip past it. After that it is enabled and focused, and the button or Enter moves on.
- If he does neither, the next sentence appears on its own 15 seconds after the button appeared. After the last sentence it goes to the end screen instead.
- Keys pressed during this time are not carried into the next sentence.

## UC10 - Reloading, leaving, giving up, and finishing

- A page load does not close the open sentence. As in the S004.1 fix after the first household test, the page sends `?do=visit` when it loads in a tab, and a background fetch of the page changes nothing.
- After a reload before the deadline, the start screen shows, and Start brings back the same sentence. It has the time left on its original deadline, the tries already used, his last submitted table, and the message from his last wrong try. Sorting he had not submitted is lost. For example, a sentence issued at 19:13:07 with a 45-second timeout must still be answered by 19:13:52 plus the allowance, however many times the page is reloaded.
- Coming back after the deadline closes the sentence as a timeout with 0 points, and the next sentence follows.
- "Try a different task" is the small button, confirmation, and S003.2 behaviour from S004.1. The timer keeps running while the confirmation is open.
- A give-up that S003.2 allows closes the open sentence as a timeout and finishes the attempt (below).
- A give-up that S003.2 refuses, because this is the only required task, leaves the open sentence alone. Unlike S004.1, it is not closed as a timeout. The deadline is fixed and a reload brings the same sentence back, so leaving it open skips nothing. The page shows the "has to be finished" note and returns to the start screen. Start brings back the same sentence with the time left on its deadline, its tries, and his last submitted table, as after a reload.
- The run finishes when every sentence has been finished, whatever the points are by then. He is asked every sentence even when a pass is already out of reach.
- When the run finishes with his points at or above `correct_answers_required`, the attempt is a pass, recorded when the last sentence finished. Today's revision count goes up by one. The "Well done" screen from S004.1 follows.
- Otherwise the attempt is a failure. The failure cooldown and the skip day from S003.1 apply. A screen says the sentences are finished and gives his points and the pass mark, such as "6.25 / 10 points. You needed 7 to pass."
- Both screens offer "Next task" or "All done for now", as in S011.
- A give-up that S003.2 allows always finishes the attempt as `abandoned`, on whichever sentence he is on and whatever his points are. The task has to be completed to count. Unlike S011, closing the open sentence as part of the give-up never turns it into a pass or a failure.
- Only finishing every sentence completes the run. A last sentence that finishes because its timer ran out, including when he comes back after its deadline, completes the run the same way as a submitted answer. The pass or failure then follows from the points. A refused give-up never completes the run, because it leaves the sentence open.

## UC11 - Keyboard and focus

- Sorting words needs a mouse. There is no keyboard way to sort a word. This is a known limitation for this child's computer, not a gap to fill with a second, conflicting control scheme.
- Tab and Shift+Tab cycle through the page's visible, enabled buttons, as in S011: Submit or Next Question, "Try a different task", and Start, the confirmation buttons, or "Next task" when they are showing. Words are not Tab stops.

## UC12 - What the browser is told

- The page is sent the sentence as words and the punctuation between them, with an id for each word, the part names in order, the try number, the time allowed and the time left, his points, the pass mark, the sentence number, and the number of sentences.
- After a wrong try it is told the two counts. When a sentence finishes it is told the outcome and the points.
- It is never told which words belong in which part, or how many words belong anywhere. S006.3 changes this for a wrong second try and for a finished sentence.

# Attempt Record

The attempt's `detail` from S003.1 holds `settings`, `runs`, and `last_activity` as S004.1 describes. Each run also records its sentence order. `questions` holds every sentence issued in this attempt, in order. Each one has:

- the run number
- the sentence's position in the task's `sentences` list, the sentence text, and its part names in order
- the expected part for every word, or none
- each try: the placements as received, the number of wrong and missed words, which words were wrong and which were missed, whether it came from the timer running out, and the time it was received in milliseconds from issue
- the outcome: `correct`, `incorrect`, or `timeout`
- the try that was correct, 1 to 3, for a `correct` outcome
- the points scored: 1, 0.5, 0.25, or 0
- the time allowed in milliseconds
- the household-local time the sentence was issued

Refused submissions are not recorded. S003.3 shows this record as it is. S006.4 adds a formatted view.

# Notes

Depends on S006.1 for the sentence configuration, on S004.1 and S011 for the shared quiz record and page shell in `policy/quiz.py` and `policy/quiz_page.py`, and on S003.2 for the task picker and give-up.

The server decides every outcome. The page only draws what the server says, so a clumsy drag cannot award points the rules would refuse. Automated tests drive the server with its clock under test control for the deadline, the network allowance, refused submissions, and the sentence order. The drag state is kept in plain functions, separate from the pointer and animation code: where each word is, and whether Submit is enabled. Those functions are tested through `tests/page_harness.js`, extended as needed. The drag flow is also checked in a real browser with real mouse drags, not DOM events sent from a script.

S006.1 keeps the task body's keys in the order written, so `policy.parts_of_speech.parse_settings` returns the parts in the parent's row order. The attempt `detail` is still stored with sorted keys, so the copy of the settings that `QuizRecord` keeps on the attempt loses that order. This story must keep the row order on the attempt too, such as by storing each sentence's part names as a list, so a resumed run shows the rows in the order the parent wrote them.

Unlike the other quizzes, a page load does not close the open sentence. It stays open until its fixed deadline. `QuizRecord.visit` closes the question left showing, so this quiz needs its own rule there.

The three-try points are the same as skip counting. They replace the problem statement's rule of 0.5 points for at least one right word in every row.

Pointer events are used, so a touch screen may also work, but the household computer has a mouse and only the mouse is required. The 1-second flight, the 1-second "Correct!" pause, the 3-second guard, and the 15-second wait are constants in the code, not configuration.

## Change Summary

A run asks every sentence once, in an order shuffled at the start of the run and stored on the run. A sentence's deadline is fixed when the server issues it. A page load, a `?do=visit`, and a refused give-up leave the open sentence open. Only its deadline, an expired run, or an allowed give-up closes it. `?do=question` brings back the open sentence with the time left, the try number, his last submitted table, and the counts from his last wrong try. An allowed give-up always records `abandoned`.

Each try is judged on the server. It counts wrong words (sorted into a part the task does not list them under, including no part) and missed words (listed but not sorted). The usual 1, 0.5, and 0.25 points apply, with the timer and late-arrival rules from S011. The page is told the sentence as words and the text between them, and the part names. It is never told the expected parts. An empty table and the table of the last wrong try are refused without using a try, unless the timer sent them or they arrived late. Bad words, unknown parts, a word in two parts, and a stale sentence are refused too.

The page keeps the table in plain functions (`place`, `remove`, `same`, `canSubmit`, `drop`, `wrongText`, and `progressText`), exposed as `window.partsOfSpeech` for the harness. The pointer code drives them. A drag starts after the pointer moves 4 pixels with the button down. Rows are found with `elementFromPoint` under the pointer. Flights are a 1-second CSS transition on a fixed-position copy. Pointer capture means releasing outside the window is still seen.

Found during browser checking: the page script declared its own `land()` function, which silently replaced the shared shell's `land(element)`, so Tab stopped moving focus. It is now `arrive()`, and a test fails if any quiz script declares a function the shell already declares.

Two small differences from the story. First, the timer bar empties at the time allowed, and the extra half second of network allowance is left for the timer's submission to arrive in. Second, the sentence number in the header moves on as soon as a sentence finishes, while "Correct!" or "Next Question" is showing.

Checked in a real browser with real mouse input (Playwright) against a local policy server at 1280 × 900. The 25-word sentence wrapped onto three lines with no sideways scrolling. Empty cells were 68 px tall against a 60 px sentence line. Hover shading was `rgb(229, 231, 235)` and the row highlight `rgb(219, 234, 254)` on both the label and the cell. A 3-pixel wiggle did not start a drag. The copy started exactly over the word, with a transparent background. Flights took 1036 ms back to the sentence and 992 ms back to a cell. The two `dry` words sorted independently, and each flew to its own place. Enter worked with the focus on the page body, and Enter on an unchanged table did nothing. A correct second try scored 0.5 and moved on after 1.05 s. Three wrong tries ended on "Not quite." with dragging locked, and Next Question was disabled for 3 s and then focused. The timer kept running while the give-up confirmation was open, and Submit was disabled meanwhile. A reload brought back the sentence, his table, and the message. The run ended on "The sentences are finished / 1.5 / 3 points. You needed 3 to pass." Sounds were checked by URL in the tests and not listened to.
