# User Voice

As a child, I want to see which words I got wrong after my second go, and the right answer after my last go, so that I can fix my mistakes myself and learn from the ones I couldn't fix.

# Use Cases

## UC1 - Red marks after a wrong second try

- After a wrong second try, the server tells the page which words are wrong and which are missed, as well as the counts S006.2 already gives.
- Every wrong word turns red (`#dc2626`) where he can pick it up from. For a word in the wrong row, that is its place in the table. For a missed word, it is the word in the sentence.
- Words he sorted correctly stay black, and he can still move them.
- A red word stays red while it is dragged. When he drops it somewhere other than where it was on the second try, it turns royal blue (`#4169E1`), as a changed box does in skip counting. "Somewhere" is a row of the table, or back in the sentence when the drop is outside the table. If he puts it back where it was on the second try, it is red again.
- Only the red words change colour. A black word stays black wherever he moves it.
- After a reload before the deadline, the red words are shown red again where they were on the second try.

## UC2 - The answer after a wrong third try or a timeout

- When a sentence finishes wrong, the server tells the page the answer: the part each word belongs in, or none. It also says, for each listed word, whether he had it in the right row, had it in another row, or did not sort it, and which words he sorted that belong in no row.
- A wrong third try shows "Not quite. Here is the answer." in place of S006.2's "Not quite." A timeout shows "Time ran out. Here is the answer."
- The table now shows the correct answer. Each row lists the words that belong in it, in sentence order, all in black text, with these backgrounds:
  - pale green (`#dcfce7`) for a word he had in this row
  - pale red (`#fee2e2`) for a word he had in a different row
  - pale orange (`#ffedd5`) for a word he did not sort
- A word he had sorted that belongs in no row, such as `the` under `noun`, is shown struck through on pale red in the row he put it in, after that row's correct words.
- The sentence goes back to plain black text with nothing red.
- The answer stays on screen while "Next Question" is showing, as S006.2 describes: greyed out for 3 seconds, then the button or Enter moves on, or the next sentence appears on its own after 15 seconds.

# Acceptance Criteria

The examples use the fox sentence from S006.1, with nouns `fox` and `dog`, verb `jumped`, and adjectives `quick`, `brown`, and `lazy`.

- [x] Two wrong tries with `fox` in `verb` and `dog` left out turn `fox` red in the `verb` row and `dog` red in the sentence. The correctly sorted words stay black and can still be moved.
- [x] A red word stays red while dragged and turns royal blue when dropped somewhere else, including back in the sentence. Dropped back where it was on the second try, it is red again. A black word stays black wherever it is moved.
- [x] Reloading after a wrong second try and pressing Start shows the red words again in the same places.
- [x] After a wrong third try with `fox` in `verb`, `dog` left out, and `the` in `noun`:
  - the `noun` row shows `fox` on pale red, `dog` on pale orange, and a struck-through `the` on pale red
  - the `verb` row shows `jumped` on pale green
  - the `adjective` row shows `quick`, `brown`, and `lazy` on pale green
  - all of it is in black text, the message says "Not quite. Here is the answer.", and the sentence is plain black
- [x] A timeout shows the same kind of answer with "Time ran out. Here is the answer."
- [x] The answer stays on screen until "Next Question", Enter after the first 3 seconds, or the 15-second wait moves on.
- [x] In the browser's developer tools, which words are wrong and missed appears only in the response to a wrong second try, and the answer appears only once the sentence is finished.
- [x] The red and blue colours and the answer display are checked in a real browser with real mouse drags.

## Change Summary

After a wrong second try, the server sends the positions of the wrong and missed words. The page records where each one was on that try: its row for a wrong word, the sentence for a missed one. A marked word is red while it is where it was on the second try and royal blue anywhere else, shown wherever he can pick it up. Unmarked words stay black. The dragged copy takes its source's colour. A reload sends the marks again, and the page rebuilds them from the restored table.

When a sentence finishes as `incorrect` or `timeout`, the server sends the answer from the last try's table. For each word it gives the part it belongs in, where he had it, and `had`: `right`, `other`, `missed`, `extra`, or `null`. The page clears the red and blue, says "Not quite. Here is the answer." or "Time ran out. Here is the answer.", and fills each row with its words in sentence order. Each word has black text on pale green (`right`), pale red (`other`), or pale orange (`missed`). Struck-through `extra` words on pale red come at the end of the row he put them in. The answer stays until Next Question moves on, as in S006.2.

Checked in a real browser with real mouse drags (Playwright) against a local server. After a wrong second try with `fox` in verb and `dog` left out:

- `fox` was red in the verb row and `dog` red in the sentence, with everything else black. The dragged copy of `fox` was red.
- Dropped in noun, `fox` turned blue. Back in verb it was red again, and sent back to the sentence it was blue.
- `dog` turned blue when sorted and red again back in the sentence. `jumped` stayed black when moved.
- A reload brought back red `dog` in the sentence and red `fox` in verb.

A third try with `the` in noun showed, under "Not quite. Here is the answer.":

- noun: `fox` on pale red, `dog` on pale orange, and `the` struck through on pale red
- verb: `jumped` on pale green
- adjective: `quick`, `brown`, and `lazy` on pale green

All of it was in black text, and the sentence was plain black.

## Suggested Acceptance Testing

### Acceptance test cases for S006.3

Use the fox sentence from S006.1 in a `parts_of_speech` task, run `docker compose up -d --build decisions`, and open it on the child's computer.

1. Make a wrong first try, then a second try with `fox` in `verb`, `dog` left out, and the rest right. Confirm `fox` is red in the verb row, `dog` is red in the sentence, and the other words are black and can still be moved.
2. Drag the red `fox` and confirm it stays red while dragged. Drop it in `noun` and confirm it turns royal blue. Drag it back to `verb` and confirm it is red again. Drag it out of the table and confirm it is blue in the sentence. Sort `dog` into a row and confirm it turns blue, then drag it back out and confirm it is red. Move a black word to another row and confirm it stays black.
3. After a wrong second try, reload and press Start. Confirm the red words are red again in the same places.
4. Make a wrong third try with `fox` in `verb`, `dog` left out, and `the` in `noun`. Confirm "Not quite. Here is the answer." and these rows, all in black text, with the sentence plain black:
   - noun: `fox` on pale red, `dog` on pale orange, and a struck-through `the` on pale red
   - verb: `jumped` on pale green
   - adjective: `quick`, `brown`, and `lazy` on pale green
5. Let the timer run out on a wrong table. Confirm "Time ran out. Here is the answer." and the same kind of coloured answer.
6. Confirm the answer stays on screen while Next Question is greyed out for 3 seconds, then moves on with the button or Enter. Left alone, it moves on after 15 seconds.
7. In the browser's developer tools, confirm `wrong_words` and `missed_words` have values only in the response to a wrong second try and the `?do=question` after one. Confirm the `answer` appears only in the response that finishes a sentence wrong.
