# User Voice

As a parent, I want to see which parts of speech he confuses across his attempts, such as adverbs sorted as
adjectives, and which words he gets wrong, and to read any one attempt sentence by sentence and try by try, so that I
can talk those parts and words through with him.

# Behaviour

## Confusions page

A **placement** is where he put one word on one try: the row he dropped it in, or **left out** if he did not sort
it. Each try gives one placement for every word in the sentence, including words he left out. A sentence of six words
answered in two tries gives twelve placements.

A word **belongs in** the part the attempt recorded for it. There is one exception. When the task has a
`parts_of_speech` filter, the attempt records a word from any other part as belonging in **no part**. For example, an
adverb in a noun, verb, and adjective task is recorded that way. For such a word, the page looks up the sentence in
the run's pools as they are now. If the sentence is still there with the same words, the word belongs in the part
the pool gives it, such as adverb. If not, it stays in no part.

A sentence's **pool now** is the pool that lookup finds it in, or None when it is not found. Parts are grouped
ignoring case, so `Noun` and `noun` are one part.

- [ ] `/parent/attempts/parts-of-speech` shows the placements in the chosen client group's parts of speech attempts in
      the period that pass the filters. It has the Period, Set, and Outcome controls from S035.1 in its address.
- [ ] Every try counts, including tries in expired runs and in abandoned, unfinished, and in-progress attempts, and a
      try the timer sent, whose unsorted words are left out. A sentence that timed out with no try counts nothing.
- [ ] A **Task** filter lists the group's parts of speech tasks and works as the words page's Task filter does in
      S035.4.
- [ ] A **Pool** filter lists the pools now of the counted sentences, and None, starts with everything chosen, is in
      the address, and keeps only the sentences whose pool now is chosen.
- [ ] A **Try** filter (1st, 2nd, 3rd) starts with everything chosen, is in the address, and keeps only the placements
      in the chosen tries. With only 1st chosen, a sentence right on its second try counts only its first try.
- [ ] With `Parts of Speech` filtered to noun, verb, and adjective, an adverb from a pool that marks it `adverb`, put
      in the adjective row, counts as adverb put in adjective, not as no part put in adjective.
- [ ] A grid has a row for each part the placements belong in, then no part, and a column for each part they were
      put in, then left out. Each cell shows its number of placements as text and is shaded by its share of its row,
      and a key explains the shading. Right placements are on the diagonal, and no part with left out.
      *Changed in implementation:* the parts are in alphabetical order on both sides, and each right cell is ringed, so
      the right cells stand out where the grid is not square.
- [ ] Each row's header shows the part's **Right**: the share of its placements in sentences whose table had that row
      that were put in it, as a whole percentage. A part that was never a row in the counted tries, such as adverb
      when every task leaves it out, shows "not asked" in place of Right.
      *Added in implementation:* no part's Right is the share of its placements left out.
- [ ] Pointing at a cell, or focusing it, shows a readout naming the pair, such as "adverb put in adjective: 7 of 31
      adverb placements", and the arrow keys move between cells.
- [ ] Clicking a cell, or Enter on it, chooses that pair, and the choice is in the address. Below the grid, its
      placements are listed newest first, each with the sentence with that word marked, the attempt's start date, the
      task, and the try. Each opens its attempt's page.
- [ ] A parts of speech task's trend page from S035.1 links to the confusions page with only that task chosen, keeping
      the client group, period, and filters.
- [ ] At phone width the grid and the word list scroll inside their own boxes, and the page does not scroll sideways.
- [ ] With no placements to show, the page says, for example, "No parts of speech sentences for younger in this
      period."

## Word list

- [ ] A list under the grid has one row for each word, folded as the quiz compares words, and the part it belongs in,
      such as `dry (verb)` and `dry (adjective)`. Its columns are Word, Belongs in, Sentences (asks of the word in that
      part), Placements, Right (as in the grid's rows), Most often instead (the wrong row or left out it was put in
      most, with its count, such as `adjective ×3`), and Last misplaced (the household day).
      *Changed in implementation:* leaving out a word whose part was not a row is not counted as wrong, in Most often
      instead or Last misplaced, as it is not in Right.
- [ ] It is sorted by Right, smallest first, then Placements, largest first. Each column header sorts by that column,
      and the sort is in the address.
- [ ] Clicking a word, or Enter on it, chooses it, and the choice is in the address. Below the list, the sentences that
      asked it are listed newest first, each with the attempt's start date, the task, and where he put it on each try,
      such as `adjective, adverb`. Each opens its attempt's page.
      *Changed in implementation:* where he put it shows every try of the sentence, whatever the Try filter.

## Parts of speech attempt page

- [ ] A parts of speech attempt's page shows a summary of its last run: points against the pass mark, such as
      "6.25 / 7 points", Questions against Maximum, sentences right on the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd try, Wrong, Timeouts, Time
      answering, and Average per right answer. Each figure S035.1 defines matches it.
- [ ] The run's sentences are listed in order, each with its outcome, points, time taken, and time allowed, then each
      try as the sentence with every word labelled by the row he put it in, marked right or wrong by an icon as well
      as colour. A wrong word shows the part it belongs in, such as `quickly adjective ✗ (adverb)`, and a word left out
      that belongs in a row is labelled "left out". A word in no part that he left unsorted has no label. In "Dry the
      dishes with a dry towel.", each `dry` is labelled and judged by its own row.
      *Changed in implementation:* the part after a wrong word is the one the attempt recorded, so in a task whose filter
      leaves adverb out, `quickly` put in adjective shows `(no part)`, as the quiz judged it.
- [ ] A word is judged as the quiz judged it. A word the task's filter left out of the rows and he left unsorted is
      right.
- [ ] A try the timer sent is labelled "Timer ran out". A sentence that timed out with no try says so.
- [ ] Each word links to the confusions page with that word chosen, for the attempt's client group and Past month.
- [ ] Each expired run is listed after the last run, under a heading with the time it expired, with the same summary
      and sentences. It does not count toward the last run's summary.
- [ ] An attempt in progress shows the summary and the sentences so far, the open sentence with its tries so far. The
      "Given up and carried on" section and the Stored record section work as in S035.2.

## MCP

- [ ] A read-only `get_parts_of_speech_confusions` tool takes a client group, the attempts filters, and the Task, Pool,
      and Try filters, and gives the grid, each part's Right, and the word list, as the page shows them. Naming a pair
      or a word adds its placements.
- [ ] `get_attempt` gives a parts of speech attempt's runs, with each try's placements judged, as its page shows them.

# Notes

Depends on S035.1 for the controls, the figures, and the trend page, S035.2 and S035.4 for the shaded grid, sortable
list, chosen-item address, Task filter, Fold, and attempt runs, S031 for sentence pools, and S036.1 for the MCP server.
Replaces S006.4, whose summary is carried over with the figures defined once in S035.1, and whose per-part table is
replaced by the marked sentences, which follow the capitalisation attempt page of S040.2.

Placements are worked out from each try's stored `placements` and each question's stored `words` when the page is
asked for, like S035.2's facts. A run's settings snapshot holds only the filtered parts, so a word the filter hid is
recorded as in no part, and only the pool now can say what it is. Every task in the current policy has a filter, so
without the pool lookup the confusion this story is for, such as adverbs put in the adjective row, would show only as
no part. The sentence is looked up by its text among the pools the run stored, and its words must match the stored
words, so an edited sentence falls back to no part. The attempt page keeps the quiz's judgement and does not use the
pool now.

Every try counts, not only the first, so a word he moved wrongly twice weighs more than one right at once. A later
try places every word again, including the ones already right, which is why the Try filter is there: 1st alone shows
his first guesses, and 2nd and 3rd show what he still got wrong after the red marks.

Right counts only placements where the part was a row, because leaving an adverb unsorted when adverb is not a row says
nothing about whether he knows what an adverb is. The grid's columns are only the rows he was offered, so it need not be square.

Out of scope: the same confusions for capitalisation, a page of its own for each word, and a filter on a sentence's
level of difficulty.

Sized against S035.4 (21 points), which had the same parts: a page of figures across attempts with Task and Pool
filters, a grid, a sorted list, a chosen item, an attempt page with runs, and an MCP tool. This story swaps This
week's words and the tiles for the Try filter, the pool lookup, and a second chosen item.

## Change Summary

Placements are worked out from each stored try when the page is asked for. The data endpoint takes the period, the
Set and Outcome filters, and the Task, Pool, and Try filters. It sends the grid, the word list, and every counted
sentence once, with each word's part and where each word went on every try. The chosen pair, the chosen word, and the
sort are the page's own and are found from those sentences, so choosing them does not ask for the data again. Their
address keys are `from_part` and `to_part` for the pair, since `from` and `to` are the Custom period's, and `word` and
`part` for the word. `no_part`, `left_out`, and `no_pool` stand for no part, left out, and None.

The pool lookup reads the pools the run's settings name, by the pool files' names, beside the policy file. A sentence
is matched by its text and then its words, and the first of the run's pools that holds it is its pool now. A pool that
cannot be read, or a sentence in it that does not parse, is skipped, so the sentence falls back to no part and None.
The Pool filter's values are the pools' ids, and its choices are the pools now of the sentences that pass the other
filters, so turning a pool off does not take it out of the list. Every sentence is looked up, filtered or not, so the
Pool filter works for every task. A part's label is the spelling seen most often among the attempts.

Without a pair or word named, `get_parts_of_speech_confusions` leaves out the sentences and each word's asks. With
`belongs` and `put`, it adds the pair's placements, each with the sentence and the word in square brackets. With
`word`, and `part` if given, it adds the sentences that asked each matching word.

The pages were checked in jsdom only. The layout, the grid's shading and rings, and the scrolling at phone width have
not been checked in a browser.

## Suggested Acceptance Testing

### Acceptance test cases for S035.3

Rebuild and start with `make up`, and sign in to the parent pages. The checks need `younger` to have a parts of
speech task with `parts_of_speech: [noun, verb, adjective]` and a pool that marks adverbs, such as one holding "The dog
ran quickly." and "Dry the dishes with a dry towel.", with a week of attempts that put an adverb in the adjective row,
a sentence right on its second try, a try the timer sent, a sentence that timed out with no try, a run that expired,
an abandoned attempt, and one in progress.

1. **An adverb put in adjective.** From the task's trend page, follow Parts of speech confusions. Check the adverb row,
   its "not asked", the adverb put in adjective cell, its readout by pointer and by Tab and the arrow keys, and Enter on
   it. Open a placement's attempt. Covers the grid, Right, the readout, the chosen pair, and the trend link.
2. **What counts.** Compare the grid's counts with the attempt pages for the expired run, the timer's try, the timed
   out sentence, and the abandoned and in-progress attempts. Covers the Every try counts check and the filter lookup.
3. **Filters.** Try 1st alone against 2nd and 3rd, each Pool choice, and each Task choice, with a task name removed
   from the policy. Edit a sentence in its pool on the Parts of Speech Pools page and reload: its adverb moves to no
   part and its pool to None. Open a period with nothing in it. Reload after each choice. Covers the Task, Pool, and
   Try filters, the pool now, and the empty message.
4. **Word list.** Check each column for `dry (verb)` and `dry (adjective)` against the attempt pages, the default
   order, each header's sort, and choosing a word. Covers the Word list area.
5. **Parts of speech attempt page.** Open an attempt with an expired run, and one in progress. Check the summary against
   the trend's readout, the labels, ticks, and crosses, "left out", "Timer ran out", the timed out sentence, each `dry`,
   a word's link to the confusions page, Given up and carried on, and Stored record. Covers the attempt page area.
6. **Phone width.** Open the confusions page in the browser's phone view over Past month. The grid and the word list
   scroll in their own boxes and the page does not scroll sideways. Covers the phone width check.
7. **MCP.** With an agent connected, call `get_parts_of_speech_confusions` with and without `try`, `pool`, `belongs`
   and `put`, and `word`, and `get_attempt` on a parts of speech attempt, and compare them with the pages. Sign in as a
   reviewer and open the confusions page. Covers the MCP area.
