# User Voice

As a parent, I want one portal with a consistent layout, a sidebar to move between pages, and a plain sign-in screen,
so that checking attempts and usage feels like using one application. The policy editor, the task editor, and the
analytics planned for later should have a place to go.

# Use Cases

## UC1 - Sign in

- The parent opens the portal at `https://<this machine>:POLICY_PORT/parent/`, or at a bookmarked page inside it,
  with no session.
- The portal shows a plain sign-in screen. The logo, the username and password fields, and the Login and Reset
  buttons are grouped in the centre of the window. It has no sidebar, context bar, or status bar.
- Login with the correct username and password opens the page the parent asked for. If they asked for no particular
  page, it opens the portal home.
- An incorrect password or an unknown username shows the message "Username or password incorrect.". The parent stays on the sign-in screen.
- Reset clears both fields and any failure message and puts the cursor back in the username field. It does not leave
  the screen. The sign-in screen has nothing to go back to.
- With no parent account, the sign-in screen says the portal is closed until an account is added. It shows no fields.

## UC2 - Move around the portal

- Once signed in, every portal page uses the same layout:
  - The navigation sidebar runs down the left side for the full height of the window. It has a midnight navy
    background, antique gold text, and the Daywarden logo centred at the top.
  - The main pane fills the rest of the window. From top to bottom it has the context bar, the content pane, and the
    status bar.
  - The context bar and the status bar are narrow soft teal strips across the full width of the main pane. Each is one
    line of text or small controls high.
  - The content pane sits between them, takes most of the screen, and has a warm ivory background with midnight navy
    text.
- The sidebar lists Attempts and Usage. The page that is open is marked in the list.
- The bottom of the sidebar shows who is signed in and has a Sign out button.
- Every navigation entry, and every link inside a page, is a normal link with an `href`. Open in a new tab, middle
  click, Ctrl-click, copy link address, and copying a link as rich text all work as they do on any web page. A plain
  click changes the content pane without reloading the whole page.
- Back, Forward, and Reload work. Every page, including a chosen client group, usage view, or attempt, has its own URL
  that can be bookmarked, pasted into another tab, or sent to the other parent.

## UC3 - Resize and collapse the sidebar

- The parent drags the right edge of the sidebar to make it wider or narrower.
- It is never narrower than 12.5rem, which is 200px at the default font size. It is never wider than a third of the
  window, unless a third is less than 12.5rem. Then it stays at 12.5rem.
- The logo scales with the sidebar width up to a maximum size, so a wide sidebar does not get a huge logo.
- The drag handle can be focused with the keyboard, and the left and right arrow keys resize the sidebar.
- The width the parent chose is kept on a reload and when the portal is opened again in the same browser.
- When the window is made narrower, the sidebar shrinks so it stays within those limits.
- A chevron button near the top of the sidebar collapses it to a thin midnight navy strip down the left edge. The main
  pane widens to fill the space.
- The collapsed strip shows only the chevron button, pointing the other way, to expand the sidebar again, and the Sign
  out button. The logo, the navigation entries, the username, and the drag handle are hidden. The strip cannot be
  resized.
- Expanding brings the sidebar back at the width it had before it was collapsed.
- Whether the sidebar is collapsed is kept on a reload and when the portal is opened again in the same browser. It
  also stays the same while moving between portal pages.
- The chevron and Sign out buttons can be reached with Tab, work with Enter and Space, and say what they do in a
  tooltip and to a screen reader, such as "Collapse sidebar", "Expand sidebar", and "Sign out".

## UC4 - Scroll only what needs to scroll

- The sidebar, the context bar, and the status bar never scroll away. The browser window itself does not scroll.
- Long content scrolls inside the content pane.
- The column headers of a list of records, such as the attempt list or a usage table, stay visible at the top of the
  content pane while the rows scroll beneath them.
- If there are more sidebar entries than fit in the window, they scroll inside the sidebar.

## UC5 - Review attempts in the portal

- Attempts in the sidebar opens the attempt review from S003.3 in the content pane. The page shows the same
  information as today. That is today's score, the required tasks outstanding, the potential tasks available, the
  potential passes waiting, and the group's attempts newest first, each with its task, set, start, finish, outcome,
  and duration.
- The client group pull-down is in the context bar. Choosing a group changes the URL, such as
  `/parent/attempts?client=younger`. Back returns to the previous group.
- A Record parent check button in the context bar records a `parent_check` pass for the client group on show. It
  cannot be used until a group is chosen. The status bar confirms the pass, and the new pass appears in the list.
- The status bar shows how many attempts are listed and the timezone the times are in.
- Each task name in the list is a link to that attempt, such as `/parent/attempts/42`.
- An attempt page shows what it shows today: the summary, any times he gave it up and came back, the answers, and the
  stored record. The context bar shows where the parent is, such as Attempts › younger › spelling-week-3. Attempts and
  the group name in it are links back to the list.
- An attempt number that is not stored shows "That attempt is not stored." in the content pane.

## UC6 - Report usage in the portal

- Usage in the sidebar opens the usage report from S008.4 in the content pane. It shows the same information as
  today, for a site group, a client group, or a quota.
- The Show, Client group, Site group, and Quota pull-downs are in the context bar. Changing them changes the URL,
  such as `/parent/usage?view=quota&quota=youtube`, and the content pane follows.
- The status bar shows the timezone the times are in.

## UC7 - Sign out and session expiry

- Sign out ends the session on the service, not only in this tab. The sign-in screen appears. Back does not show
  protected data again.
- When the session runs out from the idle limit or the lifetime in S007.1, the next thing the parent does shows the
  sign-in screen. After signing in again, the parent is back on the page they were on.
- After sign-in, the portal always opens a real portal page that has the full layout. It never opens a data request
  made in the background, such as the JSON behind the attempt list. That holds even when such a request is the one
  that found the session had run out. Data requests are always tied to the page that made them:
  - While moving to another page, such as clicking Usage, the return address is the page being opened.
  - For an action on the current page, such as Record parent check, the return address is the page on show, with its
    choices.
  - If there is no page to tie a data request to, sign-in opens the portal home.
- An action that was refused because the session had run out is not repeated by itself after sign-in. The parent sees
  the page and can do it again. A parent check is never recorded twice because of a sign-in.
- An open portal tab that nobody touches makes no requests that keep the session alive. Leaving it open does not
  extend the idle limit.

## UC8 - Old addresses

- Existing bookmarks to `/usage`, `/attempts`, and `/attempts/<number>`, with their query strings, redirect to the
  same page in the portal.
- `/` on the parent port redirects to the portal.

# Notes

## Scope

This story is the portal shell, the new sign-in screen, and the existing attempts and usage pages moved into it. It
adds no new reports or editors.

This story is only the parent portal. Changing the layout or design of the child's task pages is out of scope and is
not wanted. Those pages keep their simple, clean layout with large fonts and controls. They do not share the portal's
React build, stylesheet, palette, or layout. Any later refresh of the task pages is a separate story. S007.2 and S007.3 build their editors as pages in this portal. S004.6 and S006.4 add
their type-specific review to the portal's attempt page. They should be refined after this story is done.

A phone-sized layout, such as a sidebar that folds behind a menu button, is not in this story. The portal is used in
a desktop browser window. A later story can add a narrow-window layout once the shell has been lived with.

The portal home, `/parent/`, opens Attempts for now. A dashboard or analytics page can take over the home address
later.

## Front end

- React with React Router, built with Vite. TypeScript is suggested. Pin exact versions in `package.json` and commit
  `package-lock.json`, in line with the exact pins for Python packages.
- Use React Router's `<Link>` and `<NavLink>` for navigation, so every link renders an `<a href>`. A control that
  starts an action, such as Sign out or Record parent check, is a `<button>` and looks like a button, not a link.
- Pull-down choices live in the URL query string through the router's search parameters. They are not component
  state, so Back, Reload, and copied URLs all keep them.
- The router uses `/parent` as its base path.
- Keep text contrast at WCAG AA, 4.5:1 for body text. Gold on navy is about 7.8:1, navy on teal about 7.2:1, and navy
  on ivory about 12:1. Muted lavender (`#777E9C`) with navy text is only about 3.9:1, so use it for borders, hover, and
  selected-row tints, not as a background behind body text. Whether the content pane is ivory or white is expected to
  change after the parent sees it. That should be a one-line change in the theme stylesheet.
- Make a web-sized logo from `daywarden-logo-big-transparent.png` for the sign-in screen, and use it or a crop of
  `daywarden-logo-big.png` for the navy sidebar, whichever reads better. Make a favicon from the owl. The large
  originals stay in the repository root and are not served.
- Store the sidebar width and whether it is collapsed in `localStorage`. Clamp the width on load and on window
  resize. Collapsing keeps the stored width, so expanding can restore it.
- Use the [Lucide](https://lucide.dev/) icon library, through the `lucide-react` package, for every icon it has.
  Examples are the collapse and expand chevrons (`ChevronLeft` and `ChevronRight`, or `PanelLeftClose` and
  `PanelLeftOpen`), Sign out (`LogOut`), and the sidebar entries. Use another icon source only when Lucide has
  nothing suitable, and say why in the change. Lucide icons are bundled into the build, so the no-outside-loads rule
  still holds. A button with only an icon has an `aria-label` and a `title`.
- The collapsed strip could later show an icon for each sidebar entry, with the label as a tooltip. That is not in this
  story. The strip shows only the expand chevron and Sign out.
- Use `position: sticky` headers inside the content pane's scroll container for lists of records. A page with no
  column headers, such as a future dashboard, scrolls as a whole.
- Adding a page later should mean adding a route and a sidebar entry. The sidebar may grow headed groups, such as
  Reports and Settings, when there are enough entries to need them.

## Stylesheets and future themes

Selectable themes are not in this story. Nothing here offers a theme choice. The stylesheets are laid out so that a
later story can add themes by adding stylesheets, without reworking the portal's components:

- Split the CSS into layout and theme. Layout stylesheets hold structure, sizes, spacing, and type scale. They hold no
  colours. One theme stylesheet holds every colour. This story ships one theme, the palette above.
- Use two layers of CSS custom properties:
  - Palette properties name the theme's raw colours, such as `--palette-navy: #17243A`. They are used only inside the
    theme stylesheet.
  - Role properties say what a colour is for, such as `--sidebar-bg`, `--sidebar-text`, `--sidebar-active-bg`,
    `--bar-bg`, `--bar-text`, `--content-bg`, `--content-text`, `--border`, `--row-hover`, `--focus-ring`,
    `--button-bg`, and `--button-text`. The theme maps each role to a palette colour. Components and layout CSS use
    only role properties.
- No component, layout stylesheet, inline style, or TypeScript file names a colour value, hex or otherwise. A new
  colour that a page needs is a new role property in the theme. Lucide icons use `currentColor`, so they follow the
  role colour of the text around them.
- Define the theme's role properties under one selector that a later story can switch, such as
  `:root[data-theme="daywarden"]`. Apply the default theme's values to plain `:root` too, so a page with no
  `data-theme` attribute still has colours. A later theme is then a second
  stylesheet with its own `data-theme` value. Switching themes means changing that attribute or swapping the theme
  stylesheet, and no component changes.
- Anything a theme might want to change beyond plain colours also goes through the theme. Examples are the sidebar
  logo variant for a light or dark sidebar, shadows, and the `color-scheme` the browser uses for its scroll bars and
  form controls. The sidebar logo comes from a role property such as `--sidebar-logo`, or from a theme-selected
  asset, not from a fixed path in a component.
- Later charts and analytics read their series colours from role properties too, so they follow the theme.
- Do not use a CSS framework or CSS-in-JS approach that compiles colours into class names or component code. Such
  colours could not be swapped by loading another stylesheet.

## Service

- The decisions service serves the built portal under `/parent/` on the HTTPS listener. Any `/parent/...` path that is
  not a file and not under `/parent/api/` returns the portal's `index.html`, so deep links and Reload work. An unknown
  path under `/parent/api/` is a JSON 404. It is never the portal page. `index.html` is `no-store`. Hashed
  asset files may be cached.
- The portal shell and its assets carry no household data. Attempts, usage, the household's group names, and the
  signed-in username come only from JSON endpoints under `/parent/api/`. Those endpoints need a valid session from
  S007.1. With no session, they return 401 and no data. With no account, they return 403 and no data. Over plain HTTP,
  they are refused like the parent pages are today.
- Sign-in, sign-out, and parent check are JSON `POST`s. Keep the cookie `SameSite=Lax`, and refuse a state-changing
  request whose body is not `application/json`. That way a form on another site cannot record a pass with the
  parent's cookie.
- Sign out deletes that session's row, so the key cannot be used again.

## Design invariant: sign-in returns to a page

After sign-in, the parent always lands on a portal page. They never land on a data address. Every part of the design
keeps to this, and later portal stories must too:

- A data endpoint never redirects to the sign-in screen. With no valid session, it answers 401, or 403 with no account.
  The browser does not follow that answer anywhere. Only the portal's own code reacts to it.
- The portal decides the return address. It never uses the address of the request that failed. All data requests go
  through one shared fetch helper, and each request carries the page it belongs to:
  - A request made by a React Router loader is tied to the page being opened. The loader's `request.url` is that page's
    URL, not the data endpoint's URL. A 401 throws a redirect to `/parent/login?next=<that page>`.
  - A request made by an action, such as Record parent check, is tied to the page on show, including its query string.
  - A request that has no page, if one is ever added, sends `next` as nothing, so sign-in opens the portal home.
- The service checks `next` before it uses it, as a second guard. A return address is used only if it is a path under
  `/parent/` and not under `/parent/api/`, `/parent/assets/`, or any other file path. It must not point back to the
  sign-in screen itself. Anything else returns to the portal home. This covers a hand-made or stale sign-in link, and
  it keeps sign-in from sending the browser to another site.
- Sign-in only navigates. It does not replay a request that was refused. An action, such as a parent check, is done
  again only when the parent presses the button again. A sign-in therefore never repeats a write.
- If a later story makes data endpoints that are not under `/parent/api/`, such as React Router framework mode's
  `.data` requests, it adds them to the excluded paths above. Data addresses must always be told apart from page
  addresses by their path.
- The portal makes no timed or background requests. A request happens only when the parent acts, so the idle limit
  in S007.1 keeps its meaning.
- The server-rendered HTML pages in `policy/report.py` and `policy/review.py` are replaced. Their data assembly and
  formatting helpers, such as `format_duration` and `format_span`, can feed the JSON endpoints. Whether the endpoints
  send formatted strings or raw values for the page to format is an implementation choice. The page must show the
  same values as today.

## Build and packaging

- The decisions image is built in two stages. A Node stage runs `npm ci` and `npm run build`, and the Python stage
  copies in only the built files. The image and the `make dist` package then need no Node at run time, and the target
  machine still never builds an image.
- The decisions build context is `./policy` today. Either put the portal source in `policy/portal/`, or move the build
  context to the repository root with a `.dockerignore`. Whichever is chosen, the `lib/policy/` copy that `make dist`
  makes for `cut-clips` must not include the portal source, `node_modules`, or the build output. Add `node_modules/`
  and the portal build output to `.gitignore`.
- A Vite dev server with a proxy to the HTTPS parent port is useful for working on the portal. That is a convenience
  for development. It is not part of what is accepted.

## Tests

- Python tests cover the JSON endpoints: the session rules, 401, 403, the plain HTTP refusal, sign-out ending the
  session, the parent check recording only with a session and a JSON body, the `next` check refusing data, asset,
  sign-in, and outside addresses, the fallback to `index.html` for deep links but not for `/parent/api/` paths, the redirects from the old addresses, and the same data the old pages showed.
- Portal tests with Vitest and React Testing Library cover the routing. That includes the right page for each URL,
  sidebar entries rendered as anchors with `href` and the active one marked, pull-down choices written to the URL,
  a 401 from a loader setting `next` to the page being opened, a 401 from an action setting `next` to the page on
  show and not repeating the action, Reset clearing the form, the sidebar width clamp, and collapse and expand. That
  includes the stored state, the width coming back on expand, and the strip showing only the expand chevron and
  Sign out.
- `make test` runs the portal tests and skips them when `node` is missing, as other Node-backed tests do today.
- Existing tests that assert on the old HTML, such as `tests/test_usage_report.py`, `tests/test_policy_report.py`, and
  parts of `tests/test_parent_signin.py`, are rewritten against the endpoints and the portal. They are not deleted
  without replacing what they cover.
- The visual layout, the colours, the sticky headers, and the scrolling are accepted in the browser. They are not
  tested in jsdom.

### Durable design document

The rules in this story outlast it. Later portal stories, such as S007.2, S007.3, S004.6, S006.4, and future analytics,
must follow them without rereading this ticket. Write them down in `docs/parent-portal.md` as the portal's lasting
design reference. Describe the portal as built, in present tense. Write rules, not history, and keep this story's
acceptance details out. Include at least:

- **Scope.** The portal is the parent side only. The child's task pages are separate and keep their simple layout with
  large fonts and controls. Portal styling, components, and build never apply to them.
- **Layout.** The sidebar, context bar, content pane, and status bar, and what belongs in each. Only the content pane
  scrolls, and record lists keep their column headers in view. The sidebar's width limits, its collapsed strip, and its
  stored state.
- **Links and addresses.**
  - Every navigation is an `<a href>` that behaves like a normal link. Every action is a `<button>`.
  - Every page and every choice on it has its own URL, with choices in the query string.
  - Portal pages live under `/parent/` and data under `/parent/api/`. Data addresses are always told apart from page
    addresses by their path.
  - A new page is a new route and a sidebar entry.
- **Sessions and data.**
  - The invariant that sign-in always returns to a portal page, never to a data address. That includes how a data
    request is tied to its page, the server's check of `next`, and that a refused action is never replayed.
  - Data endpoints answer 401 or 403 and never redirect.
  - The shell carries no household data.
  - The portal makes no background requests that would extend the session.
- **Styling and themes.**
  - Layout stylesheets are kept apart from the theme stylesheet.
  - Palette properties feed role properties, and only role properties are used outside the theme.
  - No colour values in components or TypeScript.
  - A `data-theme` hook is ready for future themes.
  - WCAG AA contrast for text. The current palette, with where muted lavender may and may not be used.
- **Icons and controls.** Lucide icons through `lucide-react`. Icon-only buttons have an `aria-label` and a tooltip.
  Controls work from the keyboard.
- **Self-contained.** No fonts, scripts, images, or icons loaded from outside the parent port.
- **Build.** The portal is built inside the decisions image, and the installed package needs no Node.
