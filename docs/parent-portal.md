# Parent portal

The parent portal is the parent's side of Daywarden: one React Router app, served by the decisions service on the HTTPS parent port at `/parent/`. These rules are binding for every parent page. A change that breaks one of them changes this document in the same change, and the Parent Portal section of `AGENTS.md` too when its summary changes.

## Scope

- The portal is the parent side only. It holds the sign-in screen and every page a parent uses: the attempt review, the usage graph, the task editors, and later more analytics.
- The child's task pages under `/tasks/...` and `/quiz` are not part of the portal. They keep their own simple layout with large fonts and large controls. The portal's components, stylesheets, palette, layout, and build never apply to them, and portal work does not change their layout or design.
- The one piece of code the portal and the child's pages share is `policy/static/word_sort.js`, the drag-and-drop that sorts words into a table, used by the parts of speech pool editor and the child's parts of speech page. It is plain JavaScript outside the portal. The portal imports it, with `word_sort.d.ts` beside it, and the child's page inlines it into its own script. It marks elements only with `data-` attributes, and each page styles those with its own palette, so a change to it is checked on both pages.

## Layout

Every signed-in page has the same frame:

- **Sidebar.** Down the left side for the full height of the window. The logo is centred at the top, then the page entries, then who is signed in and Sign out at the bottom. The entry for the open page is marked. Entries that do not fit scroll between the logo and the bottom cluster, and the logo and the bottom cluster stay in place. The sidebar may grow headed groups, such as Reports and Settings, when there are enough entries to need them.
- **Main pane.** The rest of the window, from top to bottom:
  - **Context bar.** One line high, across the full width of the main pane. It holds what the page is about and the choices for it: the page title or a breadcrumb such as Attempts › younger › spelling-week-3, the page's pull-downs, and buttons that act on what is on show, such as Record parent check. A page with more filters than fit on that line, such as the usage graph, puts them on a second line in a `bar-filters` group, which wraps onto a third line in a narrow window. The title and the page's buttons stay on the first line.
  - **Content pane.** Takes most of the window and holds the page itself.
  - **Status bar.** One line high, across the full width of the main pane. It holds facts about what is on show, such as how many records are listed and the timezone of the times, and confirms or refuses the last action.

Scrolling:

- The browser window never scrolls. The sidebar, the context bar, and the status bar never scroll away. Only the content pane scrolls.
- A list of records keeps its column headers at the top of the content pane while its rows scroll beneath them, using `position: sticky` inside the content pane's scroll container. A page with several record lists, such as one table per day, keeps each list's headers in view while that list scrolls. A page with no column headers, such as a dashboard, scrolls as a whole.

Sidebar size:

- The parent resizes the sidebar by dragging its right edge, or by focusing that edge and using the arrow keys. It is never narrower than 12.5rem and never wider than a third of the window. When a third of the window is less than 12.5rem, it stays at 12.5rem. The width on screen is clamped on load and whenever the window is resized.
- The logo scales with the sidebar up to a maximum size.
- A chevron near the top collapses the sidebar to a thin strip that shows only the expand chevron and Sign out. The strip cannot be resized. Expanding brings back the width from before.
- Collapsing and expanding the sidebar animate, as every expand and collapse in the portal does. See Motion below.
- The chosen width and whether the sidebar is collapsed are kept in `localStorage`, so they last across page changes, reloads, and reopening the portal in the same browser. Collapsing keeps the stored width.

Sidebar groups:

- An entry that holds other entries, such as Task Administration, is a `<button>` with `aria-expanded`, because it expands and collapses its list rather than going anywhere. Each entry in its list is a real link, indented beneath it. The list expands and collapses with the shared animation.
- Whether a group is expanded is kept in `localStorage`, as the sidebar width is. A group is always expanded while one of its pages is open, with that page marked, and opening one of its pages keeps it expanded afterwards.
- The collapsed sidebar strip does not show a group's list.
- A reviewer's sidebar is Attempts and the Usage group only. See Access control below. The bottom of the sidebar names the role, such as "Signed in as nana (reviewer)". That line is a link to the Account page, `/parent/account`, for both roles.
- The groups are Usage, Task Administration, Asset Administration, Policy, and Settings. Policy holds Client Groups, Site Groups, Time Windows, Quotas, Rule Sets, and Household, at `/parent/policy/client-groups`, `/parent/policy/site-groups`, `/parent/policy/time-windows`, `/parent/policy/quotas`, `/parent/policy/rule-sets`, and `/parent/policy/household`. Each edits its whole section of `config/policy.yaml` and its included files at once. Its data address is `/parent/api/policy/<page>`: a GET reads the section as the files write it, and a JSON POST saves every change on the page as one change through `PolicyChanges` in `policy/policy_admin.py`, the write path the MCP tools use too. The page sends only the items it changed, with the names to delete, and the `version` it was opened on; files changed since are refused with 409. The four item pages are one piece of code (`ItemsPage` in `src/pages/Policy.tsx`) and Household is the other. Alt+N adds an item to the end of a page, Delete marks an item to be removed at the next save with Undo to take the mark away, and a new item's name is editable until it is saved. Rule Sets is a list of every rule set with its rule count and its users, and each rule set has its own page at `/parent/policy/rule-sets/<name>`, which edits its rules with one recursive rule editor (`RuleSetPage` in `src/pages/RuleSets.tsx`). Both read and save through `/parent/api/policy/rule-sets`, which also carries each rule set's users and the names the pull-downs offer. A rule set's page sends it only when its rules changed, with each rule set made by Make Named Rule Set, as one save; a rule that means what the file says is sent as the file wrote it, so nothing else is rewritten. New Rule Set and Delete Rule Set are written at once, as the request's one create or one delete. Rename, on each saved item and on a rule set's page beside Save, is written at once too: it asks for the new name in a `NameDialog`, which stays open with the reason when the name is refused, and it is disabled with a title saying why while the page has unsaved changes. It posts `{version, name, to}` to `/parent/api/policy/<page>/rename`, where `PolicyChanges.rename` changes the item's key and every reference to it in every policy file, moves the rows stored under the name (`policy/rename_history.py`), reloads the services, moves the rows once more for anything filed under the old name meanwhile, and commits it all as `renamed client group <old> to <new>`. A failure puts every file and row back, and a 5xx answer is shown in the status bar instead of the dialog. MCP has no rename tool. A rule is moved by dragging its handle (the browser's own drag events, within its own list) or with Alt+Up and Alt+Down. Settings holds MCP Server, `/parent/settings/mcp`, with its activity log at `/parent/settings/mcp/activity`. Asset Administration holds what tasks draw on, and is separate from Task Administration. It has two pages. Parts of Speech Pools, `/parent/assets/parts-of-speech-pools`, is the list of every parts of speech sentence pool, which links to each pool's own page at `/parent/assets/parts-of-speech-pools/<id>`. Capitalisation Pools, `/parent/assets/capitalisation-pools`, is the same list for capitalisation pools, with pages at `/parent/assets/capitalisation-pools/<id>`. Both lists, and both kinds of pool page, are one piece of code that takes the kind of pool, so the list, New pool, rename, delete, sign-in, and data addresses (`/parent/api/parts-of-speech-pools` and `/parent/api/capitalisation-pools`) work alike. A pool's sentences are edited on its page. A capitalisation pool's page has a row per sentence, with a text box, a Level of Difficulty box that shows the pool's as its placeholder, and a delete button, then Add sentence (Alt+N). Pasting several lines into a sentence box puts the first in that row and each other non-empty line in a new row below it. An empty sentence, one with no capital letter, one that is in the pool twice, and a level of difficulty outside 0.5 to 10 are shown under the row and stop the save; a saved sentence with no level of its own is written as a plain string. A parts of speech task's page no longer edits sentences inline: it chooses the task's pools and parts of speech filter, sets its timeout and question counts, and links to each chosen pool's page. A capitalisation task's page, `/parent/tasks/capitalisation/<name>`, is the same without the filter, and its empty Timeout shows `60s`. New task offers the Capitalisation type, and the Task Administration list shows capitalisation tasks with the others.

The sign-in screen, Forgot password, Reset password, and Password help are the pages without the frame. Each is the logo and a panel grouped in the centre of the window (`SignInPanel` in `src/pages/Login.tsx`), which Password help widens for its text. The sign-in screen holds the username and password fields, the Login and Reset buttons, and, with email on, a Forgot password link. With no parent account, all four show the closed portal message.

The portal is laid out for a desktop browser window. A narrow-window layout is not part of it yet.

## Links and addresses

- Every navigation is a real link, an `<a href>` rendered by React Router's `<Link>` or `<NavLink>`. Open in a new tab, middle click, Ctrl-click, copy link address, and copying a link as rich text all work. A plain click changes the content pane without reloading the page.
- Every action, such as Sign out or Record parent check, is a `<button>` and looks like a button, not a link.
- Every page and every choice on it has its own URL. Choices, such as a client group or a usage view, live in the query string through the router's search parameters, never in component state. Back, Forward, Reload, a bookmark, and a URL sent to the other parent all open the same page and choice.
- Portal pages live under `/parent/`, and the router's base path is `/parent`. Data lives under `/parent/api/`. A data address is always told apart from a page address by its path. Built files live under `/parent/build/`, with the favicon and icons at the top of `/parent/`. `/parent/assets/` is not built files: it holds the Asset Administration pages.
- The service returns the portal's `index.html` for every `/parent/...` address that is not a built file and not under `/parent/api/`, so a deep link and Reload work. `index.html` is sent `no-store`. Hashed files under `/parent/build/` may be cached for good. A missing file under `/parent/build/` is a 404, not the portal page. An unknown address under `/parent/api/` is a JSON 404, never the portal page.
- An unknown portal page address shows a not-found message in the content pane, with the frame around it.
- The portal never sends the browser to an address outside the portal, with one exception: Approve and Deny on the MCP consent page, `/parent/settings/mcp/connect?request=<id>`, send it to the return address the agent registered, with a code or `error=access_denied`. Both wait for the parent to press them; loading the page approves nothing. A request with a problem, such as no PKCE `S256` or a return address the agent did not register, shows the problem and sends the browser nowhere.
- Every portal page is sent with `Content-Security-Policy: frame-ancestors 'none'` and `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, so no portal page, the consent page above all, can be shown in a frame on another site.
- A new page is a new route under the layout in `policy/portal/src/routes.tsx` and an entry in the sidebar in `policy/portal/src/components/Sidebar.tsx`.

## Editing pages

A page that edits something, such as a task page, follows these rules:

- Changes are made on screen first, and nothing is written until the parent saves. Save is a button at the right-hand end of the context bar. Ctrl+S anywhere on the page saves too, and never opens the browser's save dialog. One save sends every unsaved change on the page at once.
- Save is disabled while nothing has changed, and Ctrl+S then does nothing. While there are unsaved changes, an Unsaved changes marker sits beside Save in the context bar.
- A changed field or row is marked until it is saved. The status bar confirms a save, or refuses it and lists each reason, such as each error a policy check found.
- Fields are labelled in words, such as Pass Cooldown. Each label's title is the policy file's field name, such as `pass_cooldown`, which is what the errors name.
- Every editable field that holds a date is a `yyyy-mm-dd` text box with a calendar beside it, `DateField` in `src/components/DateField.tsx`. The calendar opens with its button or Alt+Down, marks the household's today in the policy's `timezone`, and opens and closes with the shared animation. Text that is not a real day is a problem under the box that blocks Save. The browser's own `<input type="date">` is not used.
- A choice of any number of options, such as a task's client groups, is a pull-down list. Closed, it shows the chosen options separated by commas. Open, the chosen options are highlighted, and one click, Space, or Enter turns an option on or off. It opens and closes with the shared animation.
- A choice of one option that needs an icon beside each, such as a spelling task's directories, is a single-choice pull-down, `SelectOne` in `src/components/SelectOne.tsx`. A native `<select>` cannot show the icons. Its keys and clicks are the many-option pull-down's, except that choosing an option closes it. Open, the chosen option is highlighted with a tick. A choice that can also make a new entry ends with one more entry under a divider, such as New directory…, with its own icon. It closes the pull-down and opens a small dialog, `NameDialog` in `src/components/NameDialog.tsx`, with the name box focused, OK, and Cancel. The service's check of the name is the only one. A refused name keeps the dialog open with the name as typed and the reason under the box. Cancel or Escape makes nothing and puts the focus back on the pull-down. A new entry made this way is made at once and is not part of the next save, but choosing it is.
- A context bar choice of pages, such as a spelling task's Words menu, is a button that opens a menu of real links with the shared animation. The arrow keys move between the links, Enter follows one, and Escape or a click elsewhere closes it. A plain click may ask first, such as before throwing away work in progress, but middle click and Copy link address still work, and the page the link opens asks the same question.
- With unsaved changes, following a sidebar link, Back, and Forward ask whether to leave and lose the changes, in the portal's own dialog with Stay as the default. A choice that only changes the query string, such as a filter, keeps the changes and does not ask. Reload and closing the tab get the browser's own question.
- An action that cannot be taken back, such as Retire, asks first in the same dialog, with the choice that changes nothing as the default. An action that is done at once, as Retire is, is not part of the next save.
- A field that cannot be changed yet, such as every field of a new task before its name and type are locked in, or every field but the name while a task is renamed, is shown disabled. The fields that are disabled together sit in a `fieldset` with the `lock-area` class, which draws nothing itself.
- A save that needs a decision first, such as a warning that it does not ask a group to do a task again, asks in the same dialog before anything is written.
- A page that builds up work in progress on the service before a final step, such as a spelling task's words before Apply, follows the same rules for the work in progress: Save or Ctrl+S keeps it, and leaving with unsaved changes asks. The final step, and anything that acts on what is saved, such as recording, is a separate button that is disabled while there are unsaved changes and says why in its title. After the final step, or throwing the work away, the page opens the page it belongs to, with the outcome in that page's status bar.

## Recording dialogs

A page that records from the microphone, such as the spelling word list, uses `RecordDialog` in `src/components/RecordDialog.tsx`. It is given the lines to say, and how to write the clips the parent keeps.

- The dialog asks for the microphone when it opens. A refusal, no microphone, or a recorder that will not start is said in the dialog, and nothing is recorded.
- Any key starts recording, except in a form field. The recorder starts `START_DELAY_MS`, 500ms, after the key, showing "Get ready…" meanwhile, so the clatter of the key is not recorded. During that pause every key is ignored. While recording, every key but Enter is ignored, and Enter stops. Escape closes the dialog only before anything is recorded. With a recording held, only Cancel closes it.
- The take is uploaded once, then cut with a JSON request. Cutting again with other Advanced settings reuses the take.
- The review keys, K, J, D, M, R, and B, work only during review, and never while the focus is in the Advanced section.
- The review shows the line expected and how far through the lines and clips it is, such as `3 of 18: rope (sentence). Clip 4 of 20.`, with the line's own text, such as a typed sentence, beneath it.
- Requests from the dialog use `api()` with `signedOut: "refuse"`, so an ended session keeps the take and the review in the page and asks the parent to sign in again in another tab. Nothing is sent again until the parent presses the button again.
- The Advanced section expands and collapses with the shared animation. Each field puts itself back to its `bin/cut-clips` default with its reset button, a double-click on its slider, or Alt+R, and Alt+Shift+R or Reset all puts back every field.

## Sessions and data

The portal uses the parent session from S007.1: a `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax` cookie, an idle limit, and a lifetime.

- **The shell carries no household data.** `index.html`, the scripts, the stylesheets, and the images are the same for everyone and are served without a session. Attempts, usage, tasks, the household's group names, and the signed-in username come only from JSON endpoints under `/parent/api/`. Spelling recordings the task pages play also come from under `/parent/api/`, and need a session in the same way.
- **Data endpoints answer 401 or 403 and never redirect.** With no valid session they answer 401 with no data. With no parent account they answer 403 with `"closed": true` and no data. A reviewer at an address not open to reviewers gets 403 with `"admins_only": true`, a message that the address is for admins, and no data. An unknown address under `/parent/api/` is a JSON 404 for both roles. `/parent/api/session` answers the username and the role. Over plain HTTP they are refused, as every parent address is. The browser does not follow these answers anywhere. Only the portal's own code reacts to them.
- **State-changing requests are JSON POSTs.** Sign-in, sign-out, changing your own password, asking for a reset link, saving a new password from one, the parent check, a task save, locking in a new task's name and type, creating a task, renaming one, making a spelling directory, Retire, cutting, keeping, or discarding a recording, saving, applying, or cancelling a setup of spelling words, making, saving, renaming, or deleting a parts of speech or capitalisation pool, saving or deleting a usage preset, saving a policy page, saving the MCP settings, revoking an MCP connection, and approving or denying an agent are `POST`s whose body must be `application/json`. Anything else is refused with 415, so a form on another site cannot act with the parent's cookie. A password in a query string is never read.
- **The one exception is a recording's upload.** `POST /parent/api/recordings` takes the raw audio, and only when its `Content-Type` is `audio/webm` or `audio/ogg`, with or without a `codecs` parameter. Anything else, including `multipart/form-data`, `application/x-www-form-urlencoded`, and `text/plain`, is refused with 415. A form on another site can send only those three refused types, a script on another site that sends an audio type needs a CORS preflight that the service never approves, and the `SameSite=Lax` cookie is not sent on a cross-site POST. A body over 20 MB is refused with 413 before it is read to the end. No other endpoint takes anything but JSON.
- **The Account, Forgot password, and Reset password pages (S044.2).**
  - Account, `/parent/account`, is a page in the frame for both roles. `GET /parent/api/account` answers the username, the role, and the email address or null. Change password posts `{current, password, again}` to `POST /parent/api/account/password`, which checks the current password with the same Argon2 work as a sign-in, answers 400 with the reason when it refuses, and on a change ends every other session of the account, keeps the one that sent it, and ends the MCP connections the account approved. Nothing else on the page can be changed: addresses are set only on the host. With the password rules (S046), the answer also carries `rules`, and the 400 for a wrong current password and for a new password a rule refuses is the same, `{error, link, help}`, after the same work.
  - Forgot password, `/parent/forgot`, and Reset password, `/parent/reset`, need no session. `GET /parent/api/password-reset` says whether email is on, and how long a link works. `POST /parent/api/password-reset` takes `{username}` and answers 200 with the same message for every username, and 404 when email is off. `POST /parent/api/password-reset/save` takes `{token, password, again}`, refuses a password that cannot be set with 400 whether or not the token is valid, and otherwise answers 200 with the same message for every token. Neither ever says whether an account or a token exists. Both answer before they look the account or token up, and the lookup and any email happen after the answer is sent, so its time shows nothing either. The Reset password page reads the token from the address's fragment, which a browser never sends in a request line or a Referer, and sends it only in the body of Save. Every portal page is sent with `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`.
  - **Password rules (S046).** The settings in `.env` go out as `rules` with `GET /parent/api/account` and `GET /parent/api/password-reset`: each limit or null, and the lines Password help lists. They come from `.env`, never from an account. `src/passwordRules.ts` checks length and strength as the parent types, in the words `policy/password_rules.py` uses, and Save is refused on the page while a password breaks one. zxcvbn loads with a dynamic `import()`, so its dictionaries are a separate built file that only the Account and Reset password pages load. The service checks every rule again, with the history, in `screen_password` in `policy/accounts.py`: one Argon2id hash and one check against each of `PASSWORD_HISTORY` stored hashes for every request, dummies standing in for the missing ones, and the hashing on a worker thread. A reset link is looked up, checked, and used after the answer, as before, and a refused password leaves the link working. An answer whose message ends with a help link is `{message or error, link, help}`, which `HelpMessage` in `src/components/HelpMessage.tsx` shows with `link` as a link to `help`.
  - Password help, `/parent/password-help`, needs no session, and reads only `GET /parent/api/password-reset`. Its sections `#account` and `#reset` are what Learn more on the Account page and the sign-in screen open, and it links back to the Account page.
  - The three POSTs refuse a body over 4 KB with 413 before reading it to the end, as sign-in does. Like every data address, they need JSON and HTTPS, and with no parent account they answer 403 with `"closed": true`.
  - The reset link uses the name the Forgot password page was opened with when it is one of `PARENT_NAMES`, otherwise the first of them, and always `POLICY_PORT`. The tokens, their lifetime, and the cooldown are in `policy/accounts.py`, and the handlers in `policy/account_pages.py`.
- **Sending email.** `Mailer` in `policy/mail.py` writes every email and hands it to a transport, an object with one method, `send(message)`, that returns when the server accepted it or raises `MailError` with the reason. `SmtpTransport` is the one there is. A new way of sending, such as a provider's HTTP API, is another such class in `policy/mail.py`, chosen in `transport_for` from new settings read in `mail_settings`. `Mailer` and the code that calls it do not change.
- **Sign-out ends the session on the service.** It deletes that session's row, so the key cannot be used again in any tab.
- **The portal makes no background requests.** A request happens only when the parent acts: opening a page, changing a choice, or pressing a button. There is no polling or timed refresh, so a tab left open does not extend the idle limit.

### Sign-in always returns to a portal page

After sign-in, the parent always lands on a portal page with the full frame. They never land on a data address, a file, or another site.

- The portal decides the return address. It never uses the address of the request that failed. Every data request goes through the one fetch helper, `api()` in `policy/portal/src/api.ts`, and each request names the page it belongs to:
  - A request made by a React Router loader belongs to the page being opened. The loader's `request.url` is that page's URL, not the data endpoint's. A 401 throws a redirect to `/parent/login?next=<that page>`.
  - A request made by an action, such as Record parent check, belongs to the page on show, with its query string. The action's `request.url` is that page.
  - A request that belongs to no page sends no `next`, so sign-in opens the portal home.
- The service checks `next` again before it answers a sign-in. A return address is used only when it is a path under `/parent/`, not under `/parent/api/` or `/parent/build/`, not the sign-in screen itself, and its last segment is not a file name. A scheme, a host, a `..`, or anything else returns the portal home. The portal applies the same check to what the service answers.
- Sign-in only navigates. It never replays a refused request. An action is done again only when the parent presses its button again, so a sign-in never repeats a write such as a parent check.
- A later data address that is not under `/parent/api/`, such as React Router framework mode's `.data` requests, is added to the excluded paths in both checks. Data addresses must always be told apart from page addresses by their path.

## Access control

Every parent account has one role, set on the host with `dw-account` (S043, S044.1). This section is the reference for deciding which role a new page, data address, or MCP tool is open to.

- **Admin.** Runs the system. An admin can use every page, data address, and MCP tool, now and in the future. Nothing is ever closed to admins.
- **Reviewer.** Reviews the children's work and is not involved in running the system. A reviewer reads attempts and usage data with every drill-down. The one thing a reviewer changes is recording a parent check, for work done outside the system.

The rule for new functionality:

- New functionality is admin-only by default.
- It is open to reviewers only when it reads attempts or usage data and changes nothing. A new drill-down page, a new chart of attempts, or a new usage filter is open to reviewers.
- Anything that creates, changes, or deletes stored data is admin-only. This holds even when the data is about attempts or usage, as saving a usage preset or removing attempts is.
- Anything that reads how the system is set up is admin-only, even read-only: tasks, assets such as pools and recordings, policy, and MCP settings. A spelling task's Words pages count as changing the task.
- A new reviewer write needs a story that names it as an exception, and is listed here. The exceptions are:
  - Recording a parent check (S043).
  - Approving or denying an agent on the MCP consent page. This gives the agent no more than the reviewer has, so it is not a new way to change anything.
  - Changing your own password on the Account page (S044.2). The account is always the signed-in one: a reviewer cannot use it to change another account. Resetting a forgotten password by email needs no session, and works the same for every role.

Where the role is enforced. New functionality must handle each one:

- **The service's list of data addresses open to reviewers**, `REVIEWER_ADDRESSES` in `policy/portal.py`. `PortalGate` checks every request against it, by method and route template, for endpoints in `portal.py` and `mcp_server.py` alike. An address not on the list answers 403 to a reviewer. Add an address only when it fits the rule above.
- **The reviewer sidebar**, in `src/components/Sidebar.tsx`. A reviewer sees Attempts and Usage, and the signed-in line's link to the Account page.
- **The not-available message for admin page addresses.** Each admin route is wrapped in `adminOnly` in `src/routes.tsx` (`src/session.tsx`). For a reviewer its loader and action do not run, so nothing from the page is requested, and the content pane says "This page is for admins. You are signed in as nana, a reviewer." The layout loads no task list for a reviewer. The MCP consent page is not wrapped.
- **The MCP classes a reviewer's agent may use**: `attempts` and `usage`, and read only (`REVIEWER_CLASSES` and `for_role` in `policy/mcp_settings.py`). They are masked with `mcp.yaml`'s grants when tools are listed and when each call is checked, with the approving account's role read again on every call. Any other tool is refused with "This needs an admin's approval." A new tool in those classes that changes data, or a new class, is admin-only unless its story says otherwise under the rule above.
- **Links and buttons on reviewer pages.** A page open to reviewers shows no link to an admin page and no button for an admin action to a reviewer, such as Save preset on the usage pages or the MCP Server crumb on the consent page. Use `useSession()` for the role. The not-available message is a safety net, not a design.

The service's checks are what protect the data. Hiding a link or a button is for a tidy page, not for security, and every check is tested for both roles: `tests/test_roles.py` sends every data address as a reviewer and as an admin, and `src/roles.test.tsx` opens the portal as each.

## Styling and themes

- **Layout and theme are separate stylesheets.** `src/styles/layout.css` holds structure, sizes, spacing, and type, and names no colours. `src/styles/theme.css` holds every colour. No CSS framework or CSS-in-JS that compiles colours into class names or component code is used.
- **Palette properties feed role properties.** Palette properties, such as `--palette-navy`, name the theme's raw colours and are used only inside the theme stylesheet. Role properties say what a colour is for, such as `--sidebar-bg`, `--bar-bg`, `--content-bg`, `--content-text`, `--border`, `--row-hover`, `--focus-ring`, `--button-bg`, and `--button-text`. Layout CSS and components use only role properties. A colour a page needs is a new role property in the theme.
- **No colour values outside the theme.** No component, layout stylesheet, inline style, or TypeScript file names a colour, hex or otherwise. Lucide icons draw with `currentColor`, so they follow the text around them. Charts read their series colours from role properties, as below.
- **Beyond colours.** The theme also sets anything else a theme might change: `color-scheme` for the browser's scroll bars and form controls, shadows, and the logo images through `--sidebar-logo` and `--signin-logo`. Components never name an image path for the logo.
- **Themes are ready to add.** The theme's properties are defined on `:root` and on `:root[data-theme="daywarden"]`, and `index.html` sets `data-theme="daywarden"`. A later theme is a second stylesheet with its own `data-theme` value. Switching themes changes that attribute or the stylesheet, and no component changes. Nothing offers a theme choice yet.
- **Contrast.** Body text meets WCAG AA, 4.5:1. The current palette is midnight navy `#17243A`, antique gold `#D4B477`, soft teal `#80B9C5`, warm ivory `#E9E2D2`, and muted lavender `#777E9C`. Gold on navy is about 7.8:1, navy on teal about 7.2:1, and navy on ivory about 12:1. These are the sidebar, the context and status bars, and the content pane. Navy on lavender is only about 3.9:1, so lavender is used for borders, hover, and selected-row tints, never as a background behind text. Whether the content pane is ivory or white is a one-line change to `--content-bg` in the theme.

## Motion

- Every expand and collapse animates the same way: over `--expand-duration`, 500ms, with `--expand-easing`, the curve of jQuery's swing easing (`cubic-bezier(0.37, 0, 0.63, 1)`). Both properties are set once in `src/styles/layout.css`. Nothing else names an expand time or curve, and a new panel, section, or menu that opens and closes uses these two.
- Code that waits for an animation reads the duration with `expandDuration()` in `src/motion.ts`, so it never waits longer or shorter than the CSS runs. `useExpander()` there keeps collapsing content on screen while it animates out, makes it inert so the keyboard skips it, and removes it when the animation ends.
- When the browser asks for reduced motion, `--expand-duration` is `0ms` and every expand and collapse happens at once.
- Only an expand or a collapse animates. A drag, a key press on the resize handle, and a window resize change the sidebar width at once.

## Charts

Charts are built from the small kit in `src/components/charts/`, not from a charting library. `ChartFrame.tsx` holds the parts every chart shares: the plot beside its legend, the axes, and the readout of what the pointer is on. `LineChart.tsx` is the line chart on it, and `BarChart.tsx` the horizontal bar chart, with each bar's segments stacked. `TrendChart.tsx` is the task trend's chart: one marker per attempt, shaped by its outcome and hollow for practice, with lines through the attempts in order. `Sparkline.tsx` is a small inline line with no interaction, for a table cell. A later chart type, such as a donut, is another component on the same frame.

- Scales and tick values come from `d3-scale`, and paths and stacks from `d3-shape`. React draws the SVG. Nothing draws on a canvas.
- Series colours are the role properties `--chart-series-1` to `--chart-series-8`, applied by the classes `chart-series-1` to `chart-series-8`, which set `color`. Lines, dots, bars, and legend swatches draw in `currentColor`. A ninth series and beyond reuse the colours with a dashed line.
- Labels along a time axis are text the service formats in household-local time. The browser's timezone is never used to label a chart.
- Lines, bars, and legend entries are DOM elements. Each line is focusable, with an `aria-label` naming it and its total, and the arrow keys, Home, and End move the readout along it, so the keyboard reaches everything the pointer does. Each bar is a list item whose `aria-label` names it and the value at its end.
- A chart whose series can be opened, as a usage graph line opens its top hosts, opens one on a double-click of its line, Enter on its focused line, or its legend entry, which is then a `<button>`. What is open is in the page address, as every choice is.
- A chart whose lines can be hidden, as the task trend's can, makes each legend entry a `<button>` with `aria-pressed`, and the lines shown are in the page address.
- A point that opens a page of its own, as a trend marker opens its attempt, is a real SVG `<a href>`, so middle click and Copy link address work. Only one point is in the Tab order, and the arrow keys move between them.
- A shape, not only a colour, tells a marker's meaning, with a key beside the chart.
- A shaded grid, such as the arithmetic facts grid in `src/components/FactGrid.tsx`, is a table whose cells take a level from 0 to 4 with `data-level`. Each level's background and text are role properties, such as `--fact-level-3-bg` and `--fact-level-3-text`, and each cell also says its value in text, so nothing relies on colour alone. A grid that can be shaded more than one way names the shading with `data-shade` on the grid and its key, and a shading with its own colours has its own role properties, such as `--fact-weight-3-bg` for Weight. A key beside the grid names each level. A grid wider than its page scrolls inside its own box.
- A grid of outcomes, such as the spelling word grid in `src/components/WordGrid.tsx`, marks each cell with `data-result` and draws a Lucide icon in it as well as its colours, which are role properties such as `--word-missed-bg` and `--word-missed-text`. Its key shows each icon with its words. Every cell, asked or not, can take the focus, so the arrow keys move through the whole grid and the readout can say a word was not asked.
- A line chart fills the space its page gives it. A bar chart is as tall as its rows, and measures its labels so the names and the values at the bar ends fit.
- The plot measures its own box with a `ResizeObserver` and draws at that size, so a chart fills whatever space its page gives it.

## Icons and controls

- Icons come from [Lucide](https://lucide.dev/) through `lucide-react`, which bundles them into the build. Another icon source is used only when Lucide has nothing suitable, and the change says why.
- A button with only an icon has an `aria-label` and a `title`, such as "Collapse sidebar", "Expand sidebar", and "Sign out".
- Every control works from the keyboard. Buttons are reached with Tab and work with Enter and Space. The sidebar's resize handle is a focusable separator that the left and right arrow keys move.
- Anything that can be dragged on a parent page has a keyboard way to do the same thing, such as the menu Enter opens on a word in the parts of speech pool editor.
- A page's own keys use Alt with a letter, as Alt+N adds a sentence and Alt+R resets a field in the recording dialog. Browsers keep Ctrl+N, Ctrl+T, and Ctrl+W for themselves, and Chrome never passes Ctrl+N to the page. Ctrl+S is the one Ctrl key the portal takes, for Save.

## Self-contained

The portal loads no fonts, scripts, images, or icons from outside the parent port. It uses the system font stack and bundles everything else into its build, so it works on a household network with no internet. The large logo originals in the repository root are not served. The portal uses a web-sized copy, and its favicon is cut from the owl.

## Build

- The portal source is `policy/portal/`: React, React Router in data mode, TypeScript, and Vite. Every package version in `package.json` is pinned exactly, and `package-lock.json` is committed.
- The decisions image is built in two stages. A Node stage runs `npm ci` and `npm run build`, with `policy/static/word_sort.js` copied in beside the portal as it sits in the repository, and the Python stage copies in only `policy/portal/dist`. `npm run dev` is allowed to serve that one directory outside the portal. The image and the `make dist` package need no Node at run time, and the target machine never builds an image. The `lib/policy/` copy that `make dist` makes for `cut-clips` leaves the portal out.
- `node_modules/` and `policy/portal/dist/` are not committed.
- The portal's tests use Vitest and React Testing Library in jsdom. `make test` installs the npm packages when Node is there, then runs those tests and the type check with the Python tests. Without Node they are skipped. The visual layout, the colours, the sticky headers, and the scrolling are checked in a browser, not in jsdom.
- `npm run dev` in `policy/portal` serves the portal from Vite with its data requests sent to the HTTPS parent port of a running stack.

## Capitalisation in the MCP server and the attempt page

- The MCP permission classes include `capitalisation_pools` (create, read, update, delete), beside `sentence_pools`, in `mcp.yaml` and on the MCP page. Its tools are `list_capitalisation_pools`, `get_capitalisation_pool`, `create_capitalisation_pool`, `update_capitalisation_pool` (add and remove sentences together), `rename_capitalisation_pool`, and `delete_capitalisation_pool`, each needing its permission and working as the sentence pool tools do. Sentences are written as the pool file writes them, a text or a mapping with `sentence` and `level_of_difficulty`, and a sentence with no capital is refused with the page's reason. `create_task` and `update_task` make and change `capitalisation` tasks, with `pools` as ids of capitalisation pools only. `get_attempt` returns a capitalisation attempt with each try's clicked words in its record.
- A capitalisation attempt appears in the attempts overview and trend as `capitalisation/<name>`, with the figures a parts of speech attempt has. Its page shows a summary of the last run, then each sentence in order with every try: the words he clicked, blue when they needed a capital and red when not, and each capital he missed red, the points the try earned or a timeout, and the time taken. Earlier runs follow. The blue and red are the theme's `--mark-right` and `--mark-wrong`.
